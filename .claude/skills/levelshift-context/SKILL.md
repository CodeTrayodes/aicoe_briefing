# LevelShift context

Read the full context document before generating any output:

```
context/levelshift-context.md
```

That file contains:
- Section 1: Who LevelShift is (company, offices, history, size)
- Section 2: Mission, vision, CLAPQ values
- Section 3: Full services portfolio (AI transformation, Salesforce, Microsoft, Data, Integration, BPS)
- Section 4: Key clients and case studies with real numbers
- Section 5: Target industries and verticals
- Section 6: Technology partnerships and internal tool stack (M365, GitHub Copilot, Claude, Salesforce, Boomi, Azure)
- Section 7: Competitive landscape and differentiators
- Section 8: The seven news lenses — HOW to contextualise each category
- Section 9: Output instructions — what makes a good vs bad levelshiftAngle
- Section 10: Strategic context for 2026

## What a good levelshiftAngle looks like

Good — specific, names a real asset, ends with an action:
> "LevelShift's 25+ Microsoft Fabric certified engineers and the SPINX case study
> (Microsoft-corroborated) position us to lead this conversation with retail clients
> immediately. Add this to the Fabric practice deck and reach out to the CITGO account
> team this week."

Bad — generic, could apply to any consultancy:
> "This is relevant to LevelShift's AI transformation work and something the team
> should monitor closely."

## Relevance scoring

- 9-10: Directly affects a named LevelShift service line, partner, or client
  (e.g. Salesforce Agentforce update, Microsoft Fabric release, EU AI Act deadline)
- 7-8: Strongly relevant to a target vertical or active sales conversation
- 5-6: Useful background for the team
- 3-4: Tangential — include only if no stronger story is available
- 1-2: Do not include
